# 🛠️ August 10th, 2026 - Ninth Build Session: Final Internal Fillets, Motor Selection, & Design Aesthetics

This session marked the finalization of all internal structural reinforcement, the selection of our flight propulsion hardware, and the definitive branding and aesthetic design choices for the vehicle.

## 🧪 Injection Port Finalization
I completed the injection and curing of the final 12 internal epoxy fillets. With all 36 internal port cavities fully packed with our fiber-reinforced structural epoxy matrix, the 6-fin split-fin can is officially integrated and ready to handle extreme transonic flight forces.

## 🎨 Branding & Aesthetics: The Legend of *Dead Man's Hand*
Originally, the vehicle was code-named *El Beeper* with the intention of carrying an audible tracking payload, but budget constraints required shifting priorities. Looking closely at my component bin, I realized my donated parachute features a striking red-and-black pattern reminiscent of a casino roulette wheel. 

Embracing this casino theme, I settled on a high-contrast color palette: a deep red for the nose cone and airframe switch band, with a sleek black covering the payload section, fins, and booster airframe. I narrowed the final name choices down to *High Roller* and *Dead Man's Hand* (the classic poker hand of aces and eights held by Wild Bill Hickok at his demise). I ultimately chose **Dead Man's Hand**—it brings a gritty, bold edge to my first certification attempt, though its sister project will certainly be named *High Roller* if I continue the casino theme into my Level 2 build.

## 🚀 Motor Selection & OpenRocket Flight Simulations
While people often assume the objective of high-power rocketry is to fly as high and fast as possible, true flight certification requires the opposite mindset. The purpose of a certification flight is to prove you can safely prepare, launch, and recover an H, I, or J impulse vehicle without compromising range safety. The higher a rocket flies, the more it will drift during descent—especially under a single-deployment configuration without electronic dual-recovery. Therefore, keeping the apogee modest ensures a tight recovery radius and a safe visual return.

Balancing these safety requirements with my design goals, I selected the **AeroTech H242R-M** motor. The Redline propellant formula produces a bright, vivid crimson flame that matches the rocket's color scheme perfectly, while providing the stable rail-departure velocity and predictable, conservative apogee needed for a text-book Level 1 certification.
