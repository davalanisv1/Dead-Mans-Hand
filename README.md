# Dead Man's Hand 🚀
### National Association of Rocketry (NAR) Junior Level 1 Certification Project

**Dead Man's Hand** is a high-power rocketry project built on the Mach 1 Viper 54 airframe. This repository serves as the engineering log, flight simulation data center, and code repository for the rocket's future avionics package as I work toward earning my NAR Junior Level 1 high-power certification.

---

## 📐 Vehicle Specifications

The vehicle is constructed using a  **Viper 54mm kit from Mach 1 Rocketry**. It features thin-wall fiberglass construction to balance lightweight performance with extreme structural strength.

* **Diameter:** 54 mm (2.14 in)
* **Total Length:** 58 in
* **Booster Section:** 30 in
* **Payload Section:** 16 in
* **Nose Cone:** 5:1 ogive filament-wound fiberglass with a gunmetal anodized aluminum tip
* **Fins:** High-strength G10 fiberglass with an interlocking fin matrix for precise alignment
* **Motor Mount:** 38 mm (compatible with high-impulse H, I, and J motors)
* **Recovery System:** Dual-deployment ready, featuring an electronic bay, M5 stainless steel hardware, and a 6-foot Kevlar tether
* **Launch Hardware:** 1010-size rail buttons

## 📖 Origin & Project Backstory

During the final weeks of my Sophomore year, I developed a strong desire to build. I watched endless reels of students my age making rockets, planes, participating in competitions and hackathons, and my poor experience in my high school's Robotics Team had left me even more hungry for development. So I decided to go big or go home. 

On June 1st, 2026, I pitched an ambitious concept to my engineering teacher: a remote-controlled jet based on the Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor, augmented with rocket motors to push through the sound barrier toward Mach 1.5. Because I am a minor and an L-2 Visa holder, I faced strict constraints—I could not legally hold a job to fund it, nor could I personally buy or handle high-power rocket motors without an adult supervisor to manage the grants and hazardous logistics.

While my Introduction to Engineering Design teacher admired the ambition, she recommended I connect with the school's Aerospace instructor instead. Recognizing my passion, he challenged me to build a stepping stone first—a project to develop the precision manufacturing techniques, electronics familiarity, and reputation required to chase a supersonic jet. He became my project sponsor, graciously providing a free 12-month NAR membership alongside a donated airframe, parachute, and rocket motor. 

Construction on *Dead Man's Hand* officially began on June 1st, 2026.
