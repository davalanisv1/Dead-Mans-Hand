# 🔩 June 24th, 2026 - Second Build Session: Airframe Drilling, Rail Hardware, & First Fin Set Installation

Two days after completing the core motor mount assembly, I returned to the workshop. This session focused on precision drilling of the airframe, installing internal mounting hardware, and beginning the split-fin installation.

## Airframe Drilling & Rail Hardware
We drilled a total of 37 holes into the lower booster section to accommodate the rocket's unique 6-fin split-fin design (three forward fins and three aft fins). Thirty-six of these holes—three on each side of all six fin slots—serve as injection ports for future internal epoxy fillets. The final booster hole was drilled near the bottom to house the lower 1010-size launch rail button. A separate rail button hole was drilled into the upper payload body tube to provide a wide, stable stance on the launch rail.

After drilling, I applied high-strength epoxy to the flanges of the PEM nuts (threaded inserts) to secure them inside the rail button mounting holes from the interior of the tubes. Next, I applied structural epoxy to the interior of the airframe and slid the motor mount assembly into place, aligning the rings while keeping the airframe exterior clean.

## First Fin Set Installation
Once the motor mount was secured, we moved immediately to installing the first set of fins. Proper surface preparation was critical here to ensure a permanent structural bond:
1. **Mechanical Abrasion:** Sanded the root tabs of the G10 fiberglass fins to create a textured surface profile for the epoxy to grab.
2. **Chemical Solvent Cleanse:** Wiped the sanded areas down thoroughly with acetone followed by isopropyl alcohol to fully strip away any latent grease or finger oils.

After cleaning, the root tabs were coated in structural epoxy, slid smoothly into the airframe slots, and aligned. We utilized wooden bracing sticks and a specialized 3D-printed fin alignment guide to guarantee that both the forward and aft fins were perfectly concentric and parallel down the long axis of the airframe.
